Pattern A · Three strategies for the "other links"

Pattern A's 4 verbs (BUY · RENT · SERVICE · PARTS) absorb the catalog actions. These three strategies show how to place the leftover items — Locations / Customer Login / Employee tools / About / Sponsorships / Jobs / Resources / Contact — without sliding back into 25-item chaos.

1 · Tight utility + footer directory 2 · 5th WHEELER ▾ menu 3 · Search-led, minimal chrome
Strategy 1 · Tight utility + footer directory RECOMMENDED

Three persistent utility items. Brand directory in the footer.

Top utility row stays at 3 items: Locations · Customer Login · For Employees ▾. Brand items (About / Sponsorships / Jobs / Resources / Contact) move to the footer, where every other industrial-dealer site keeps them. The "For Employees ▾" dropdown audience-codes the internal/admin tools so customers don't get confused.

Pros

  • Header chrome: 4 verbs + 3 utility + CTA = 8 items (vs current 25). Honest hierarchy.
  • Audience-coded utility solves the customer-vs-staff Login confusion.
  • Footer brand directory matches dealer-industry convention (Cat OEM, peers).
  • Locations gets first-class billing — high-priority intent for field users.
  • Yellow CTA is unambiguously the primary action.

Cons

  • About / Sponsorships / Jobs require a footer scroll for first-time visitors.
  • "For Employees ▾" dropdown is a hover/focus interaction (touch needs a tap-to-open).
  • The utility row's 3 items still need labels short enough to fit on tablet portrait.

Best fit

  • The default for Wheeler. Industry-conformant + opinionated hierarchy.
  • When the footer is well-designed (currently is — captured Quick Links / Contact / Connect are solid).
  • When the brand wants customer-facing chrome to feel confident, not loaded with staff tools.
Strategy 2 · 5th WHEELER ▾ menu

Brand items stay in the header. Verb row gains a 5th identity menu.

A WHEELER ▾ dropdown sits at the end of the verb row, opening a column of brand items (About / Locations / Sponsorships / Jobs / Resources / Contact). Utility row shrinks to two items: Customer Login and Employee Login.

Pros

  • Every navigation target is in the header. No footer scroll required.
  • Brand identity (Wheeler) gets a visible top-level slot — useful if About / Sponsorships are conversion-critical.
  • Utility row is the cleanest of the three (2 login links only).

Cons

  • WHEELER ▾ is a noun in a row of verbs — breaks the cognitive pattern that makes the 4-verb model work. Users learn "this row is what I CAN DO" then hit a brand name.
  • Adds visual weight to the verb row (now 5 items instead of 4) — tighter on tablet.
  • Locations buried one click deeper than Strategy 1.

Best fit

  • When the footer is weak and can't carry brand items convincingly.
  • When Sponsorships / About / News are genuinely top-traffic pages (not the usual footer-dwellers).
  • Less recommended than Strategy 1 for Wheeler specifically.
Strategy 3 · Search-led, minimal chrome

No utility row. Search bar carries the long tail.

Drop the utility row entirely. Put a search bar in the header where utility links normally sit. Search handles long-tail discovery (find a 259D3, find a branch in Cedar City, find the Cat Card page) so the visible chrome stays the 4 verbs plus a search field and a phone CTA. Everything else lives in the footer.

Pros

  • Most premium-feeling of the three. Header reads clean and modern.
  • Search bar serves the "I know what I'm looking for" intent (model code, branch name, blog post) in one keystroke.
  • One row total — header height drops to ~96px. Hero gets maximum canvas.
  • Footer accommodates every leftover link honestly without crowding the top.

Cons

  • Requires real search infrastructure — search bar is the load-bearing piece; if it's poor (no autocomplete, no fuzzy match), the whole strategy fails.
  • Locations / Login lose first-class billing — both buried in the footer.
  • Tablet portrait gets cramped quickly: 4 verbs + search + CTA + logo on one row.
  • Less industry-conformant than Strategies 1 and 2.

Best fit

  • When users arrive with a specific model or location in mind (the "I know what I want" intent).
  • When the brand wants a premium / Apple-corporate register rather than a "browse the catalog" register.
  • When the build budget includes a real search backend (Algolia / typesense / SearchWP+).

Recommendation

Strategy 1 is the strongest default for Wheeler: keeps the header at 8 chrome items, audience-codes the staff vs customer tools, and leans on an already-strong captured footer for brand items. Strategy 2 is a fallback when the footer can't be trusted to carry brand items. Strategy 3 is the bold play that requires real search to deliver.
